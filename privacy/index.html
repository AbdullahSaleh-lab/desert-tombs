Privacy Policy — Desert Tombs

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Desert Tombs is a game for iPhone. It has no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and it contains no third-party code. This policy explains what it keeps and what it sends.

What the game stores on your device

Which chambers you have lit and how many scarab coins you found in each, your sound and music switches, the username you set in Settings, and, while a race is going, the room you are in so you can rejoin it. They are kept in the app’s own storage on your device. Deleting the app removes them.

Racing with friends

When you create or join a room, the game connects to our room server, hosted by Cloudflare. It sends the username you choose for that room, the room’s code, a random player number the game makes on your device (so you keep your seat if the connection drops), and your moves on each board. The room uses them to run the race and show the scores to the players in that room, and to no one else.

A room’s data is deleted when the room is closed, or, if nobody closes it, after two hours without play (ten minutes once everyone has left). We do not store your IP address (Cloudflare’s network may process it briefly to deliver and protect the service). Pick a username that doesn’t identify you if you prefer; it does not need to be your name.

Practice with bots runs entirely on your phone and sends nothing.

Permissions

Desert Tombs does not ask for your location, camera, photos, contacts, microphone, tracking or notifications.

Apple-provided data

The App Store gives developers aggregated, anonymous statistics such as download counts and crash reports, depending on your device’s analytics-sharing setting. These never identify you.

Children

The game is suitable for all ages. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date above will change with it.
